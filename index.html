I am building a missed-call recovery automation business for local service businesses, starting with plumbers. The service helps small businesses recover leads they lose when they miss phone calls. When a customer calls and the business misses the call, the system instantly texts the customer, alerts the business owner, logs the lead, and tracks how many leads were captured each month. The system uses Twilio, Make.com, and Airtable. Twilio receives the phone call and sends call data to Make.com through a webhook. Make.com responds immediately with TwiML XML to hang up the call so there is no timeout. Then Make.com searches Airtable to identify which client owns the Twilio number that was called. Once the correct client is found, Make creates a lead record, updates the monthly lead count, sends an SMS to the caller, and sends an SMS alert to the business owner. The system is designed to be reusable and multi-client. Instead of rebuilding the automation for every new customer, each new business can be added as a new row in Airtable. The incoming Twilio number is matched against the client’s Twilio number in Airtable. The main customer is a plumber or local service business that misses calls while working, driving, after hours, or during emergencies. These businesses often lose jobs because customers call competitors if they do not get a quick response. The main value proposition is: “Stop losing customers when you miss calls. We instantly text missed callers, notify you, and track every recovered lead so you can see exactly how many opportunities we saved.” The MVP includes: - Incoming/missed call detection - Automatic SMS to the caller - SMS notification to the business owner - Airtable lead logging - Monthly lead count tracking - Multi-client support through Airtable The current tech stack is: - Twilio for phone numbers, call webhooks, and SMS - Make.com for automation - Airtable for client data and lead tracking The Airtable database has a Clients table and a Leads table. The Clients table stores: - Business Name - Company Name - Twilio Number - Owner or plumber phone number - SMS reply message - Customer SMS template - Notify Owner? - Active? - Timezone - Notes - Start Date - Leads per Month - Lead Count Month - Monthly Fee - Plan - Status - Calendly Link The Leads table stores: - Customer phone number - Twilio number called - Business name - Company name - Call SID - Call status - Date/time - Linked client record - Lead source - Notes - Status - SMS sent status - Owner notified status The automation flow is: Customer calls Twilio number → Twilio sends webhook to Make.com → Make responds with Hangup XML → Make searches Airtable Clients table by Twilio Number → Make gets the full client record → Make updates monthly lead count → Make creates a Lead record → Make sends SMS to caller → Make sends SMS alert to business owner. Example customer SMS: “Thanks for calling {{Company Name}}. Sorry we missed you — a team member will reach out shortly. Reply with your issue and location so we can help faster.” Example owner SMS: “Missed-call lead captured. Business: {{Company Name}}. Caller: {{Customer Phone}}. Lead #{{Leads per Month}} captured this month. Call them back ASAP.” Pricing ideas: Starter Plan: $97–$197/month for missed-call SMS, owner alerts, lead logging, and lead count tracking. Growth Plan: $197–$397/month for reply forwarding, weekly summaries, and lead status tracking. Pro Plan: $397–$997/month for AI replies, scheduling, CRM-style pipeline, and analytics. The future roadmap includes: Phase 1: MVP missed-call SMS and owner notification. Phase 2: Customer reply forwarding to business owner. Phase 3: Lead status tracking such as New, Contacted, Booked, Lost, Completed. Phase 4: Weekly summaries showing leads captured and estimated recovered revenue. Phase 5: AI-powered SMS replies to collect service type, urgency, address, and appointment preferences. Phase 6: Scheduling integration through Calendly or similar tools. Phase 7: Full CRM dashboard showing missed calls, recovered leads, booked jobs, and ROI. The business goal is to create a simple monthly recurring revenue service that is easy to sell, easy to onboard, and valuable to local service businesses. The first niche is plumbers because one recovered plumbing job can often pay for the monthly service.
